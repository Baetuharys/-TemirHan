# Functional GD&T control selection

## Principle
Choose a geometric control from the function that must be protected, not from a generic list of symbols.

## Decision sequence
1. Identify the functional feature: hole, shaft, face, slot, pattern, mating surface, or profile.
2. State what can fail functionally: location, orientation, form, profile, or rotation/runout.
3. Identify the datum reference frame needed to establish functional location/orientation.
4. Select the least-complex control that constrains the identified failure mode.
5. Only introduce material-condition modifiers when the functional inspection/manufacturing logic supports them.
6. Do not invent numerical tolerances; derive or request them from functional requirements, fit, process capability, or customer standard.

## Review questions
- Is the datum scheme physically meaningful?
- Does the control constrain the intended degrees of freedom?
- Is the tolerance measurable with the planned inspection method?
- Does the drawing duplicate a size tolerance and geometric tolerance unnecessarily?
- Are MMC/LMC/RFS assumptions explicit?
