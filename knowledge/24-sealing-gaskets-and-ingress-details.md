# Sealing, Gaskets and Ingress-Control Details

## A gasket is a system, not a strip
Ingress performance depends on the entire boundary: gasket profile, compression, door/frame stiffness, corners, seams, hardware, openings, cable entries, vents, coating and assembly variation.

## Design sequence
1. Mark one closed sealing boundary.
2. List every penetration and seam crossing it.
3. Obtain the gasket supplier's compression recommendation and environmental limits.
4. Make the compression land geometrically continuous.
5. Ensure hinges/latches distribute compression rather than creating local peaks.
6. Check tolerances and door flatness against the available compression window.
7. Check drainage separately; sealing and water management are related but not identical.
8. Prototype, inspect and test.

## Common problems
- Gasket bridges a sharp corner and lifts locally.
- Weld or seam creates a bump under the gasket.
- Powder coat changes the compression stack.
- Door is too flexible between latch points.
- A vent, gland or removable plate bypasses the intended seal path.
- Compression set reduces long-term recovery.

## IP language
Geometry can be reviewed **against an IP target**, but an enclosure is not declared compliant only from CAD. The applicable ingress tests and product conformity process determine the supported claim.
